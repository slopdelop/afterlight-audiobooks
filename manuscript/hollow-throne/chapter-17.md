# Chapter Seventeen: The Emperor's Last Order

The first man through the service door was carrying a burning tool case.

Leia pulled him aside before he could drag it into the passage. Heat struck her face. A sleeve of pale flame ran along the case's seams, giving off an oily smoke that smelled of scorched wiring. The contractor tried to explain something, coughed, and lost his grip. Han caught the handle with the end of a fallen conduit and heaved the case into an empty freight recess.

"Everybody out of the junction," Leia said. "Use the stairs. Leave the equipment."

People crowded past her in orange work coveralls. They had come to remove an obsolete palace relay, one of the thousands of pieces of Imperial machinery the new administration was trying to inventory, reuse, or sell. Their work order had passed three separate safety inspections. Now a bright red line was moving across the relay room's wall display, and somebody inside was striking metal with a hammer.

Inspector Orl caught the last contractor by the shoulder.

"How many?"

The woman looked back toward the smoke. "Five of us. We're all here. The duty officer stayed."

The hammering stopped.

Leia started toward the door. Han caught her arm, then released it as soon as she looked at him. He pulled his collar over his mouth and went in beside her.

The room occupied the triangular space between two enormous power trunks. Copper bus bars ran overhead, their polished surfaces catching the red display light. A disassembled switching cabinet stood in the middle of the floor. The contractors had laid its panels on padded cloths and arranged the screws in shallow trays. Everything about their work suggested patience and care.

The maintenance officer was kneeling beside the open cabinet. He had taken off his gloves. His left hand lay flat against a metal plate while his right held a short, heavy hammer.

"Leave that," Han said. "We've got a fire."

The officer looked up. His eyes were streaming. A single drop fell from his chin onto the plate.

"I know."

He raised the hammer.

Leia caught his wrist. For a moment he seemed willing to let her take it. Then his arm drove downward with such force that she had to step inside the swing to keep her balance. The hammer struck the cabinet edge. Pain went through her hand.

"Han."

Han took the hammer shaft. The officer clung to it, his face contorted with effort.

"I can't let go," he said.

Leia looked down at his other hand. Two fingers had begun to blister against the plate.

Somewhere behind the cabinet, a speaker clicked.

"Complete the authorized disposal."

The voice was mild and official. The officer jerked toward it. Han dragged the hammer sideways, wedging its head beneath a brace, and Chewbacca came through the doorway behind them. The Wookiee took one look at the man's burning hand and reached over the cabinet. Metal tore. The speaker came away in his fist, trailing two wires.

Silence fell with an abruptness that made the alarms in the corridor sound distant.

Chewbacca caught the officer's forearm and lifted the burned hand clear. The man folded it against his chest, fighting to keep it there while his fingers tried to spread again.

"Stay with him," Leia told Han.

The red line reached the end of the display.

A cover slid open beneath the bus bars. Inside, a row of narrow cylinders began to glow.

Chewbacca pushed Leia backward. One of the cylinders burst with a hard white flash. Burning particles hissed across the cabinet. Han got the officer to his feet, ducked beneath his arm, and half carried him toward the door while Leia pulled a fire blanket from its wall clip. She threw it over the open cabinet. Something beneath it spat and crackled.

Chewbacca seized the isolation handle beside the power trunk. It would not move. He planted one foot against the wall and pulled again. A heavy contact separated with a noise like a blaster shot. The lights went out.

For a second, Leia saw only the bright image of the glowing cylinders hanging in her vision. Then emergency strips came on along the floor.

Han was in the passage with the officer. The man was trying to say something through a fit of coughing. Leia bent close enough to hear.

"There's another supply."

Behind her, the red display lit again.

***

They got everyone beyond the pressure door before the second charge fired.

It sounded disappointingly small: a sharp pop, followed by the softer impacts of things falling inside the room. Smoke pressed against the door's narrow window. Chewbacca held the locking wheel while a contractor sprayed suppressant into the ventilation intake.

The officer sat on the stairs with a medical dressing around his hand. His uniform still carried the outline of a removed Imperial badge. Leia crouched in front of him, leaving enough distance that he did not have to look up.

"What should we call you?"

He gave his name very quietly. She repeated it, making sure she had heard him correctly.

"I'm Leia. This is General Solo. We're going to have somebody look after your hand."

He studied her face. Recognition arrived before relief, and seemed to frighten him more.

"I didn't know what was in there. They told us to maintain the distribution units. They said there were people whose treatment depended on them."

Han leaned against the opposite wall. His sleeve had burned through near the elbow.

"Who told you?"

The officer looked at his bandage.

"Different people. Sometimes it was a recording. We had reviews. Medical appointments. I thought—"

He stopped. Leia waited while the contractor worked at the ventilation intake. Somewhere above them a lift bell sounded, and a man shouted that the upper passage had been cleared.

"I thought I was going to lose my position," the officer said. "After Endor. They said the reviews would help us work with the new administration."

Leia felt Han turn toward her. The same machinery that had kept Darren in service might have kept others at their posts. She could not yet tell how many, or which of the officer's memories were reliable. She could see the shape of his hand through the dressing.

"We're going to ask you about that," she said. "Somewhere quiet, with a doctor present. You can stop when you need to."

"They'll ask me to finish it."

"There won't be a speaker in the room."

The man looked at her then. She let him decide whether to believe her.

Orl came down the stairs carrying a square ceramic plate in a pair of insulated tongs.

"The Wookiee found this behind the first cabinet," he said. "He seems to believe it matters."

Across its pale surface ran four columns of tiny black marks. Part of the lower corner had cracked away. A lead seal remained attached to one edge by a wire.

Han bent toward it. "That came out of the hot part?"

"A separate sleeve. Apparently designed to survive the destruction of the records around it."

Leia recognized the central crest. She had seen it on enough death warrants and emergency decrees to know the Emperor's private household seal.

She asked Orl to put it down.

***

C-3PO's image appeared above the portable projector with a faint blue ripple.

"Princess Leia. General Solo. I understand there has been an incident."

Behind him, Lando crossed the Bespin examination room carrying a coil of cable. Commander Venn's voice asked someone to keep a circuit isolated. They were still working on the records Han had brought from Tatooine.

"We have a disposal warrant," Leia said. "I need you to read the small text beside the household seal."

She angled the projector's scanner over the plate. Threepio leaned forward as though the extra distance would help him see through the transmission.

"Oh. This is a formal cessation instrument. An old form, even by Imperial standards. The operative text is compressed into the authentication columns."

"Can somebody make one of these?" Han asked.

"Certainly, General. The question is whether it would survive examination. Commander Venn, might I impose upon you for the notary sequence we recovered?"

Venn stepped into view. She compared the transmitted marks with a display Leia could not see.

"Same signing chain," she said. "But this one's earlier than the active maintenance credentials. The seal has a physical witness impression."

"Which proves what?" Han asked.

"That it wasn't generated yesterday by someone playing the Emperor's voice."

Leia studied the lead seal. Beneath Palpatine's crest was a hairline pattern left by a tool pressed into the soft metal. Someone in his household had stood beside this instrument when it was made. The sight of it was unexpectedly intimate.

Threepio began translating.

"All local Witness operations are to cease upon receipt. Experimental command profiles are to be withdrawn from routine use. Associated reference material is to be collected under the attached custody schedule for final destruction."

Han straightened.

"He ordered it closed?"

"The language is quite explicit."

Leia looked toward the pressure door. Black residue had begun to settle on its window. Behind it lay the station that might have shown them how the palace commands were being routed.

"Then why did it tell that man to burn his own hand?"

Threepio paused. Venn moved closer to his projector.

"Let us see the lower edge," she said.

Orl tilted the plate. Part of the custody schedule had survived beside the break. Threepio read silently for several seconds.

"This instruction does not authorize the disposal of every reference here," he said. "Selected originals were to be returned to a private notary. Local operators were obliged to make their installations incapable of independent use."

"That's an elegant way to describe an incinerator," Han said.

"It is deliberately imprecise," Threepio replied. "Someone has attached a much more specific enforcement routine. I doubt the people carrying out the original warrant would have been permitted to read it."

Lando set down his cable.

"Leia, the officer said there were patients depending on that relay?"

"Yes."

"Our Bespin records mention maintenance exemptions. They kept research equipment alive under medical continuity orders. If the old command system sees a dismantling crew, it might carry out the destruction instruction while protecting anything classed as patient support."

Han looked back toward the stairs. "Including the officer who was supposed to destroy it."

"Apparently his welfare came second," Lando said.

Leia could imagine the original arrangement. A secret order, carried by people allowed to obey it but not understand it. Another instruction defining which pieces of the secret should survive. Each document bearing an authentic seal. Any doubt left to the person standing in the room when the machines began to burn.

"Is the Emperor's warrant genuine?" she asked Venn.

"Within what we can authenticate here, yes. I'd want the paired notary impression to prove its full custody."

Threepio enlarged the last intact column.

"The destination is a mobile executive notary assigned to the second battle station. Cassette Nineteen."

Han glanced at Leia.

The expedition Luke was arranging had just become necessary.

***

By the time the engineers declared the relay room safe, the contractors were angry.

Leia preferred their anger to the expression she had seen on the maintenance officer's face. They wanted to know who had signed the work order, why nobody had warned them, whether the surviving equipment would be left for another crew to discover. She stayed until Orl had copied their records and made sure they had somewhere to report injuries without having to explain how Imperial research equipment had burned them.

One of the women held up a ruined socket driver.

"That's mine. Not the government's."

Leia took its serial number herself.

Han waited until they were walking back through the freight passage before speaking.

"You can't replace every tool on Coruscant."

"I can replace that one."

He nodded, accepting it.

Beyond the open junction door, Chewbacca was kneeling in the ash with a pair of cutters. He had lifted the ceramic sleeve clear of the cabinet's wreckage and found an armored conduit beneath it. The conduit had melted where it passed through the floor. Whatever local connection they might have followed was gone.

Han examined it. He and Chewbacca exchanged a few low words, mostly in Shyriiwook. Han's expression lost its last trace of humor.

"It burned down into the service shaft," he told Leia. "The route list, the switching records, everything that would have told us where the orders came from. Somebody arranged for this to be the first thing that happened when we started taking it apart."

Leia looked at the severed end. The destruction had made their immediate work harder. It had also required an actual prepared installation, a man kept in place, and a routine someone had chosen to preserve. Veyr's reach had edges. She needed to find them before anyone else followed a perfectly reasonable work order.

"Can we protect the other crews?"

Orl had already opened his paper folder.

"I can suspend these disposal permits. The municipal crews will listen to me. Palace engineering may require a more impressive signature."

"They'll have mine."

"Good."

Han touched the broken conduit with his boot.

"And I'll get the other half of our warrant."

***

Luke answered from a plain compartment aboard Ackbar's command ship. Through the doorway behind him, Leia could see technicians securing equipment into transport racks. Artoo rolled past carrying a clamp in his extended gripper.

"Ackbar has salvage teams near Endor already," Luke said when she had explained. "They've been keeping civilian traffic out of the worst fields. A few of the larger modules ejected before the explosion. We may be able to identify the right one."

Han had brought up an old Imperial service diagram on the Falcon's small hold display. Chewbacca turned it until the notary unit's extraction mounts faced them.

"We've got its serial and the last custody beacon," Han said. "I'll send them on a direct burst. No command key attached."

Luke's eyes moved over the diagram.

"It won't be a whole section of the station."

"Don't need a whole section."

"I know. I want you to see the salvage estimates before you come."

Han studied him, then looked back at the battered extraction mounts.

"Send them."

Leia listened while they worked out a rendezvous. Han would take the Falcon to Ackbar's staging point; Luke and Artoo would join him there. Chewbacca asked a question and Han altered their equipment list. They needed external shielding for the retrieval bay, two independent tethers, and enough medical supplies for somebody who might not be aboard when they left.

When the call ended, Han remained beside the display.

"Come with us?"

Leia looked toward the folded sheet Orl had left on the table. Three clinic supply orders, paid through a relief account her father had once supported. One had been cancelled on the morning of Darren's fall. Another had been quietly redirected. Orl had promised to ask people, without entering their names into a palace terminal.

"If I leave now, whoever helped Darren may decide I've stopped looking."

Han considered the sheet. Then he turned off the display.

"All right."

She leaned against him for a moment, feeling the warmth of his unburned sleeve. He rested his hand at the back of her neck.

"Don't answer any calls from me while I'm gone," he said.

She drew back enough to see his face.

"That could make this difficult."

"Make me prove it's me."

Leia thought of a small kindness she had remembered the previous night, and of how much of a person might survive beyond the things an enemy could record. She took Han's hand and pressed it once.

"You can start by bringing this one back."

He looked down at their hands.

"Planning on it."

She stayed on the platform while the Falcon lifted into the night traffic. Then she returned to Orl's waiting shuttle. There were people below the palace who had kept one another alive under worse governments than this one. She would have to reach them without bringing the palace down on their heads.
