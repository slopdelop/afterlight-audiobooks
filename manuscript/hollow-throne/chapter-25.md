# Chapter Twenty-Five: The Hollow Throne Awakens

The security officer waiting at the receiving platform knew the challenge before Leia asked for it.

He also knew why she was asking. He came aboard without his weapon drawn, gave his name to Han and showed the small cut across his knuckle that the duty commander had described. His counterpart stayed at the outer door. Neither used a recorded voice or asked them to trust the Imperial seals on a screen.

Leia felt some of the strain leave her body. She had been holding the prism's case through the last part of the descent. When she let go, the shape of its handle remained across her palm.

The Falcon's fourteen rescued passengers were ready to move. Jessa had helped the attendants organize them into groups small enough for the platform lifts. She had asked each person which name to use. Some answers matched the manifest. Two did not. Leia told the attendants to write down what they had been given.

Lando went first, with Sovan walking beside his stretcher until a clinic medic persuaded the man to sit in a chair of his own. Chewbacca took the stretcher's rear handles. Sela stayed with Iven, who watched the opening hatch as if it might demand something from him.

Beyond the platform, a dispatch operator directed the arriving vehicles through a bank of glass windows. It was an ordinary room, crowded with chairs, thermal drink containers and people working too long at the same task. Orl had arranged to meet them here and guide the final transfers by hand. Leia looked for his small figure among the vehicles.

The security officer raised a hand toward the outer door. A warning light had come on above it.

"Hold there."

Leia stopped. He spoke into his comlink, listened, then shook his head at the officer outside. She could hear him questioning the revised route. The original plan had taken the patients directly into two independent clinic vehicles. Dispatch now wanted a short transfer through a medical decontamination lift.

"We have wounded," the officer said. "Clear the platform or give me the supervisor."

In the glass room, a woman in a grey dispatch uniform took a headset from its hook. Her left hand was trying to hold her right away from the console. Leia saw the effort before she understood it.

"Han," she said.

The outer platform doors closed.

***

The first shot struck the security officer's comlink.

He fell sideways against a loading post. Leia caught his arm and pulled him behind the stretcher as Chewbacca swung it clear. The patient's blanket slid away from Lando's immobilized shoulder. He tried to turn toward the sound; Sovan pressed him down with a hand that shook almost as badly as his own.

Two dispatch orderlies stood beside the decontamination lift. One carried a blaster in a medical supply bag. The other had a long pole with an insulated hook, the kind used to recover equipment from a live power channel. Behind the glass, the woman at the console was crying. The platform's old Imperial maintenance speaker continued delivering instructions in the calm voice of a man who was not present.

The armed orderly's muzzle rose toward Leia. His eyes were wide with terror. There was no time to decide how much of his movement was his own. She fired a stun bolt and saw him crumple beside the lift.

The second orderly pulled the manual separation handle.

A barrier dropped between Leia and the Falcon's hatch. Han threw himself backward as the lower edge struck sparks from the deck. Chewbacca caught the stretcher before it tilted and drove it toward the inner loading recess, where Sela had taken the patients out of the line of fire.

Leia moved after them. The pole's hook caught the harness beneath her jacket.

It dragged her sideways hard enough to wrench the breath from her. She fired at the pole's shaft. The stun bolt spattered harmlessly against its shielded casing. The orderly pulled again, using the lift's recovery winch to take the load.

The strap tightened across her ribs. On the other side of the barrier, Han was cutting at a service panel. Leia could hear the Falcon's alarm through the metal.

The second security officer reached the glass room and forced its door open. A man inside struck him with the butt of a weapon. The officer fell out of sight. The woman at the console kept trying to lift her hand.

Leia turned toward the winch. If she could reach its mechanical release, the case would come free with her. Between them stood the orderly with the hook. He did not look at her face. His free hand was clenched so tightly that blood ran from his palm.

"Let go," she said. "You can let me take it."

His arm pulled instead.

Behind her, Lando's stretcher struck a loose rail. One of the patients stumbled into its path. Chewbacca caught the man, leaving Sela and Sovan alone with the tilting frame. The injured security officer lay near its wheels, trying to drag himself clear.

Leia had one hand on the harness catch. She looked once toward the winch.

Then she released the strap.

The case flew across the gap, struck the lift's recovery cradle and disappeared behind its protective bars. The orderly followed it inside. A man in a black maintenance coat slammed the inner gate and pressed the descent control.

Leia fired through the bars. Her shot struck the control housing. The lift stopped after dropping half a level, its top barely visible beneath the platform rim.

She ran to the edge. The man in black had opened a second gate onto an old service conveyor. He handed the case through to someone below. The harness still hung from its side. She could see the torn thread where it had caught on her jacket.

The shot that came back struck the lip beside her hand.

Chewbacca seized her belt and pulled her away from the edge. The stretcher behind him was upright. Han had forced a gap in the barrier wide enough to slide through.

"They've got it," Leia said.

He looked toward the lift. Then he looked at the patients crowded into the loading recess.

"We can't take them down there."

"I know."

It hurt more because she did know. She had made the choice with her own hand on the catch.

Han pressed the wounded officer's uninjured hand against a dressing and helped him stand. Chewbacca forced the loading recess's manual exit. Orl appeared on the other side with a municipal tug and an expression so furious that Leia almost failed to recognize him.

"This way. The private vehicles are still clear."

"How many?" Leia asked.

"All of yours. Thirty from the Starling are already divided between two sites. The people here can go to the other two. I have their drivers in sight, and I know the drivers' mothers."

The wounded officer looked toward his fallen counterpart. Orl put a hand on his shoulder.

"A response team is coming through the dispatch office's rear door. We'll get him. You need to move before that speaker gets its operator back."

Leia helped push Lando's stretcher onto the tug. Her harness lay in the lift below them. For the first time since Darren had given her the case, its weight was gone.

***

Threepio heard the palace announcement on a clinic worker's receiver.

It declared a temporary protective administration in the name of the former Emperor's standing medical authority. It ordered selected security and dispatch personnel to maintain their posts, suspend transfers and accept the designated Custodian's instructions. Most people in the clinic regarded it as another Imperial officer refusing to understand the war was over.

Darren recognized the cadence. He tried to stand before his right leg could take his weight.

Threepio put the receiver in a drawer and closed it. The clinic worker switched off its power.

"That instrument cannot appoint a government," the droid said. "Its authority is confined to the sessions it names. It cannot make those words lawful by saying them more firmly."

Darren's fingers had closed around the edge of the bed. "It can make some of the people carrying guns believe they have to obey."

"Yes," Threepio said. "It can."

He found the copied reference notes, checked that the reader held no active command attachments and put it into Darren's coat pocket. The treadle and its copper contact fitted into Orl's tool bag. Threepio took that himself.

The corridor outside had become crowded. The clinic was receiving calls from relatives caught near a sealed palace entrance and from a delivery driver whose vehicle had been diverted into the occupied dispatch hub. The building's own systems still worked. Its staff had no reason to expect them to continue doing so if someone traced a patient's history through the municipal transfers.

Sela arrived carrying a folded blanket. There was blood on one sleeve, and Darren saw it before she could explain.

"Lando," she said. "He's alive. It's his shoulder."

"The others?"

"Out. All forty-three."

Darren's face crumpled briefly. He covered it with one hand. Sela waited beside him until he could ask the next question.

"Iven?"

She turned toward the corridor. A tall man stood there, clutching a tray with a single cup on it. The clinic worker had tried to take the tray; he had allowed her to put a blanket over his shoulders instead.

Darren made room on the bed without being asked. Iven looked at the empty space, then at Sela.

"Only for a minute," she said. "We have to leave this place."

Iven sat. He put the tray on his knees, and Darren steadied the cup for him. Neither man asked the other why his hands were shaking.

Leia entered behind Sela. She had a burn along the back of her hand and nothing beneath her torn jacket.

Darren looked there first.

"I'm sorry," she said. "They took the case at the transfer platform. It was a prepared ambush. We got the people out."

He closed his eyes.

She waited for his anger. She could have explained the barrier, the lift or the wounded man beneath the stretcher. None of those facts would make the case safe again.

"Did you bring them?" he asked.

"Yes."

He opened his eyes. "Then help me up."

***

Veyr received the prism in the fifth station's reference room.

The man who carried it had been a palace dispatch supervisor for eleven years. He had passed through three reorganizations and the fall of the Empire without being asked about the small medical notation in his personnel record. Now he stood beside the installation bench, holding his empty hands against his sides.

Veyr inspected the case's shield and the damaged harness. He asked whether anyone had opened it during the transfer.

"No, Custodian."

"Very good. You may return to your post."

The supervisor did not move. His eyes had settled on the black surfaces inside the case. A faint tremor passed through his jaw.

"Your post," Veyr repeated.

The man turned. His shoulders struck the doorframe on the way out. Veyr made a notation about the remaining delay in his response, then deleted the patient's first name from the visible heading. He preferred the session identifier while work was in progress.

Above the bench, three sections of the palace system showed active custody: the old distribution spine, the medical dispatch hub and the temporary assembly wing. Beyond them, the city continued to resist him. Municipal operators had begun routing around the occupied hub. Fleet command had rejected the announcement and isolated its palace channels. A Republic response force was securing the approaches to the west wing.

The figure on Veyr's display was much smaller than the territory he intended to hold eventually. It would have to be enough. The remaining permissions on Sim Aloo's wafer would not sustain indefinite renewal. With the original reference restored, he could begin transferring those sessions into the renewed root and expand the bindings under his own authority.

He seated the prism among its silver supports.

A delayed image from the assembly wing showed two representatives standing in front of an armed guard. One refused to sit. The other had taken off her shoes and was holding them by their straps. Neither had been conditioned. They understood the weapon perfectly well.

Veyr ordered the guards to keep the room supplied with water and to prevent any official statement that had not passed through him. He did not tell them that frightened representatives would soon endorse his administration of their own accord. The procedure required time, repeated exposure and opportunities to shape a subject's associations. He had made that clear in his staffing instructions. The room would remain a hostage room until other methods became possible.

One of the station's technicians asked whether they should abandon the outer medical hub. A cutover by independent operators was already reducing its usefulness. Holding it would expose prepared personnel to the approaching security teams.

For a moment Veyr considered the losses. The hub staff had names. He knew several of them. He had once interviewed a woman there about the care she took arranging her daughter's school transport.

His right thumb pressed twice against a stud beneath his left cuff. A wire tightened along the sleeve. At the edge of his collar, a pale contact gave one faint pulse against the skin below his ear.

The memory became an observation without weight.

"Maintain the hub until the transfer is complete," he said.

The technician returned to work. Veyr opened the renewal schedule. Its first sessions had already begun counting toward the next reference exchange.

He could still make the arrangement stable. He would remove the uncertainty from the people around him, then from the institutions that depended upon them. Nobody would be required to endure a preventable disorder merely because an individual could not bear to accept the necessary course.

He adjusted the prism's final support. The signal steadied.

***

Orl's maintenance depot stood beneath a disused public transit platform. It smelled of hot bearings and the bitter cleaner used on old electrical housings. The shutters had been closed for the night. Someone had left a half-finished cup of tea beside a model of the city's freight lifts, its surface marked by a skin that had begun to fold inward.

Lando lay on the depot's narrow inspection couch while a civilian medic stabilized his shoulder. Luke arrived before the dressing was finished. He had stayed with the Starling until the last of its passengers reached the independent vehicles, then returned when the receiving platform went silent. By that time Leia's party had already escaped it.

He stood beside the couch, listening as Han described the dispatch cell's movements. He asked about the people who had obeyed the speaker. Nobody could tell him which ones had done so willingly.

"Then we plan for both," Luke said.

Leia placed the copied patient reader on Orl's workbench. Threepio set the bag containing the treadle beside it. They had the observations Darren had permitted them to retain, the Meridian's separate care-channel measurements and the legal distinction between an active command and a record of one. Veyr had the physical authority root.

Through the depot's old receiver came a report from the west assembly wing. Republic security had established a cordon. The hostages were alive. Negotiators had demanded access and been refused.

Another report confirmed that the second officer at the transfer platform had been recovered with a fractured skull. He was breathing without assistance. The dispatch operator had survived as well. She had broken two fingers trying to keep her hand away from the barrier control.

Leia wrote both reports down. She did not want them to become anonymous qualifications beneath the account of how the prism had been lost.

Sela returned from checking the civilian sites. Orl had sent separate drivers rather than messages. Each brought back a count and a request: extra bedding, a replacement regulator, something to eat that did not come in a measured medical portion.

Forty-three Alderaanians remained outside Veyr's custody. Iven sat at the end of the bench with a warm cup between his hands. Darren rested beside the couch, his right leg supported on a box of old inspection lamps.

They had brought the people.

Now they would have to stop the machinery from reaching them again.
