# Chapter Seventeen: The Emperor's Last Order

The first man through the service door was carrying a burning tool case.

Leia pulled him aside before he could drag it into the passage. Heat struck her face. A sleeve of pale flame ran along the case's seams, giving off an oily smoke that smelled of scorched wiring. The contractor tried to explain something, coughed, and lost his grip. Han caught the handle with the end of a fallen conduit and heaved the case into an empty freight recess.

"Everybody out of the junction," Leia said. "Use the stairs. Leave the equipment."

People crowded past her in orange work coveralls. They had come to remove an obsolete palace relay, one of the thousands of pieces of Imperial machinery the new administration was trying to inventory, reuse, or sell. Their work order had passed three separate safety inspections. Now a bright red line was moving across the relay room's wall display, and somebody inside was striking metal with a hammer.

Inspector Orl caught the last contractor by the shoulder.

"How many?"

The woman looked back toward the smoke. "Five of us. We're all here. The duty officer stayed."

The hammering stopped.

Leia started toward the door. Han caught her arm, then released it as soon as she looked at him. He pulled his collar over his mouth and went in beside her.

The room occupied the triangular space between two enormous power trunks. Copper bus bars ran overhead, their polished surfaces catching the red display light. A disassembled switching cabinet stood in the middle of the floor. The contractors had laid its panels on padded cloths and arranged the screws in shallow trays. Everything about their work suggested patience and care.

The maintenance officer was kneeling beside the open cabinet. He had taken off his gloves. His left hand lay flat against a metal plate while his right held a short, heavy hammer.

"Leave that," Han said. "We've got a fire."

The officer looked up. His eyes were streaming. A single drop fell from his chin onto the plate.

"I know."

He raised the hammer.

Leia caught his wrist. For a moment he seemed willing to let her take it. Then his arm drove downward with such force that she had to step inside the swing to keep her balance. The hammer struck the cabinet edge. Pain went through her hand.

"Han."

Han took the hammer shaft. The officer clung to it, his face contorted with effort.

"I can't let go," he said.

Leia looked down at his other hand. Two fingers had begun to blister against the plate.

Somewhere behind the cabinet, a speaker clicked.

"Complete the authorized disposal."

The voice was mild and official. The officer jerked toward it. Han dragged the hammer sideways, wedging its head beneath a brace, and Chewbacca came through the doorway behind them. The Wookiee took one look at the man's burning hand and reached over the cabinet. Metal tore. The speaker came away in his fist, trailing two wires.

Silence fell with an abruptness that made the alarms in the corridor sound distant.

Chewbacca caught the officer's forearm and lifted the burned hand clear. The man folded it against his chest, fighting to keep it there while his fingers tried to spread again.

"Stay with him," Leia told Han.

The red line reached the end of the display.

A cover slid open beneath the bus bars. Inside, a row of narrow cylinders began to glow.

Chewbacca pushed Leia backward. One of the cylinders burst with a hard white flash. Burning particles hissed across the cabinet. Han got the officer to his feet, ducked beneath his arm, and half carried him toward the door while Leia pulled a fire blanket from its wall clip. She threw it over the open cabinet. Something beneath it spat and crackled.

Chewbacca seized the isolation handle beside the power trunk. It would not move. He planted one foot against the wall and pulled again. A heavy contact separated with a noise like a blaster shot. The lights went out.

For a second, Leia saw only the bright image of the glowing cylinders hanging in her vision. Then emergency strips came on along the floor.

Han was in the passage with the officer. The man was trying to say something through a fit of coughing. Leia bent close enough to hear.

"There's another supply."

Behind her, the red display lit again.

***

They got everyone beyond the pressure door before the second charge fired.

It sounded disappointingly small: a sharp pop, followed by the softer impacts of things falling inside the room. Smoke pressed against the door's narrow window. Chewbacca held the locking wheel while a contractor sprayed suppressant into the ventilation intake.

The officer sat on the stairs with a medical dressing around his hand. His uniform still carried the outline of a removed Imperial badge. Leia crouched in front of him, leaving enough distance that he did not have to look up.

"What should we call you?"

He gave his name very quietly. She repeated it, making sure she had heard him correctly.

"I'm Leia. This is General Solo. We're going to have somebody look after your hand."

He studied her face. Recognition arrived before relief, and seemed to frighten him more.

"I didn't know what was in there. They told us to maintain the distribution units. They said there were people whose treatment depended on them."

Han leaned against the opposite wall. His sleeve had burned through near the elbow.

"Who told you?"

The officer looked at his bandage.

"Different people. Sometimes it was a recording. We had reviews. Medical appointments. I thought—"

He stopped. Leia waited while the contractor worked at the ventilation intake. Somewhere above them a lift bell sounded, and a man shouted that the upper passage had been cleared.

"I thought I was going to lose my position," the officer said. "After Endor. They said the reviews would help us work with the new administration."

Leia felt Han turn toward her. The same machinery that had kept Darren in service might have kept others at their posts. She could not yet tell how many, or which of the officer's memories were reliable. She could see the shape of his hand through the dressing.

"We're going to ask you about that," she said. "Somewhere quiet, with a doctor present. You can stop when you need to."

"They'll ask me to finish it."

"There won't be a speaker in the room."

The man looked at her then. She let him decide whether to believe her.

Orl came down the stairs carrying a square ceramic plate in a pair of insulated tongs.

"The Wookiee found this behind the first cabinet," he said. "He seems to believe it matters."

Across its pale surface ran four columns of tiny black marks. Part of the lower corner had cracked away. A lead seal remained attached to one edge by a wire.

Han bent toward it. "That came out of the hot part?"

"A separate sleeve. Apparently designed to survive the destruction of the records around it."

Leia recognized the central crest. She had seen it on enough death warrants and emergency decrees to know the Emperor's private household seal.

She asked Orl to put it down.

***

C-3PO's image appeared above the portable projector with a faint blue ripple.

"Princess Leia. General Solo. I understand there has been an incident."

Behind him, Lando crossed the Bespin examination room carrying a coil of cable. Commander Venn's voice asked someone to keep a circuit isolated. They were still working on the records Han had brought from Tatooine.

"We have a disposal warrant," Leia said. "I need you to read the small text beside the household seal."

She angled the projector's scanner over the plate. Threepio leaned forward as though the extra distance would help him see through the transmission.

"Oh. This is a formal cessation instrument. An old form, even by Imperial standards. The operative text is compressed into the authentication columns."

"Can somebody make one of these?" Han asked.

"Certainly, General. The question is whether it would survive examination. Commander Venn, might I impose upon you for the notary sequence we recovered?"

Venn stepped into view. She compared the transmitted marks with a display Leia could not see.

"Same signing chain," she said. "But this one's earlier than the active maintenance credentials. The seal has a physical witness impression."

"Which proves what?" Han asked.

"That it wasn't generated yesterday by someone playing the Emperor's voice."

Leia studied the lead seal. Beneath Palpatine's crest was a hairline pattern left by a tool pressed into the soft metal. Someone in his household had stood beside this instrument when it was made. The sight of it was unexpectedly intimate.

Threepio began translating.

"All local Witness operations are to cease upon receipt. Experimental command profiles are to be withdrawn from routine use. Associated reference material is to be collected under the attached custody schedule for final destruction."

Han straightened.

"He ordered it closed?"

"The language is quite explicit."

Leia looked toward the pressure door. Black residue had begun to settle on its window. Behind it lay the station that might have shown them how the palace commands were being routed.

"Then why did it tell that man to burn his own hand?"

Threepio paused. Venn moved closer to his projector.

"Let us see the lower edge," she said.

Orl tilted the plate. Part of the custody schedule had survived beside the break. Threepio read silently for several seconds.

"This instruction does not authorize the disposal of every reference here," he said. "Selected originals were to be returned to a private notary. Local operators were obliged to make their installations incapable of independent use."

"That's an elegant way to describe an incinerator," Han said.

"It is deliberately imprecise," Threepio replied. "Someone has attached a much more specific enforcement routine. I doubt the people carrying out the original warrant would have been permitted to read it."

Lando set down his cable.

"Leia, the officer said there were patients depending on that relay?"

"Yes."

"Our Bespin records mention maintenance exemptions. They kept research equipment alive under medical continuity orders. If the old command system sees a dismantling crew, it might carry out the destruction instruction while protecting anything classed as patient support."

Han looked back toward the stairs. "Including the officer who was supposed to destroy it."

"Apparently his welfare came second," Lando said.

Leia could imagine the original arrangement. A secret order, carried by people allowed to obey it but not understand it. Another instruction defining which pieces of the secret should survive. Each document bearing an authentic seal. Any doubt left to the person standing in the room when the machines began to burn.

"Is the Emperor's warrant genuine?" she asked Venn.

"Within what we can authenticate here, yes. I'd want the paired notary impression to prove its full custody."

Threepio enlarged the last intact column.

"The destination is a mobile executive notary assigned to the second battle station. Cassette Nineteen."

Han glanced at Leia.

The expedition Luke was arranging had just become necessary.

***

By the time the engineers declared the relay room safe, the contractors were angry.

Leia preferred their anger to the expression she had seen on the maintenance officer's face. They wanted to know who had signed the work order, why nobody had warned them, whether the surviving equipment would be left for another crew to discover. She stayed until Orl had copied their records and made sure they had somewhere to report injuries without having to explain how Imperial research equipment had burned them.

One of the women held up a ruined socket driver.

"That's mine. Not the government's."

Leia took its serial number herself.

Han waited until they were walking back through the freight passage before speaking.

"You can't replace every tool on Coruscant."

"I can replace that one."

He nodded, accepting it.

Beyond the open junction door, Chewbacca was kneeling in the ash with a pair of cutters. He had lifted the ceramic sleeve clear of the cabinet's wreckage and found an armored conduit beneath it. The conduit had melted where it passed through the floor. Whatever local connection they might have followed was gone.

Han examined it. He and Chewbacca exchanged a few low words, mostly in Shyriiwook. Han's expression lost its last trace of humor.

"It burned down into the service shaft," he told Leia. "The route list, the switching records, everything that would have told us where the orders came from. Somebody arranged for this to be the first thing that happened when we started taking it apart."

Leia looked at the severed end. The destruction had made their immediate work harder. It had also required an actual prepared installation, a man kept in place, and a routine someone had chosen to preserve. Veyr's reach had edges. She needed to find them before anyone else followed a perfectly reasonable work order.

"Can we protect the other crews?"

Orl had already opened his paper folder.

"I can suspend these disposal permits. The municipal crews will listen to me. Palace engineering may require a more impressive signature."

"They'll have mine."

"Good."

Han touched the broken conduit with his boot.

"And I'll get the other half of our warrant."

***

Luke answered from a plain compartment aboard Ackbar's command ship. Through the doorway behind him, Leia could see technicians securing equipment into transport racks. Artoo rolled past carrying a clamp in his extended gripper.

"Ackbar has salvage teams near Endor already," Luke said when she had explained. "They've been keeping civilian traffic out of the worst fields. A few of the larger modules ejected before the explosion. We may be able to identify the right one."

Han had brought up an old Imperial service diagram on the Falcon's small hold display. Chewbacca turned it until the notary unit's extraction mounts faced them.

"We've got its serial and the last custody beacon," Han said. "I'll send them on a direct burst. No command key attached."

Luke's eyes moved over the diagram.

"It won't be a whole section of the station."

"Don't need a whole section."

"I know. I want you to see the salvage estimates before you come."

Han studied him, then looked back at the battered extraction mounts.

"Send them."

Leia listened while they worked out a rendezvous. Han would take the Falcon to Ackbar's staging point; Luke and Artoo would join him there. Chewbacca asked a question and Han altered their equipment list. They needed external shielding for the retrieval bay, two independent tethers, and enough medical supplies for somebody who might not be aboard when they left.

When the call ended, Han remained beside the display.

"Come with us?"

Leia looked toward the folded sheet Orl had left on the table. Three clinic supply orders, paid through a relief account her father had once supported. One had been cancelled on the morning of Darren's fall. Another had been quietly redirected. Orl had promised to ask people, without entering their names into a palace terminal.

"If I leave now, whoever helped Darren may decide I've stopped looking."

Han considered the sheet. Then he turned off the display.

"All right."

She leaned against him for a moment, feeling the warmth of his unburned sleeve. He rested his hand at the back of her neck.

"Don't answer any calls from me while I'm gone," he said.

She drew back enough to see his face.

"That could make this difficult."

"Make me prove it's me."

Leia thought of a small kindness she had remembered the previous night, and of how much of a person might survive beyond the things an enemy could record. She took Han's hand and pressed it once.

"You can start by bringing this one back."

He looked down at their hands.

"Planning on it."

She stayed on the platform while the Falcon lifted into the night traffic. Then she returned to Orl's waiting shuttle. There were people below the palace who had kept one another alive under worse governments than this one. She would have to reach them without bringing the palace down on their heads.

# Chapter Eighteen: The Wreck of the Second Sun

At first Han thought the dark line crossing Endor's clouds was a shadow.

Then the Falcon's range display settled, and he understood how far away the objects were. Pieces of the battle station filled a band of space wider than the distance between most planets and their moons. Sunlight caught an occasional surface and turned it silver. Everything else remained black against the forest world below.

He had flown out of that explosion. He had spent the intervening months enjoying the fact that it was behind him.

"Tell me you brought a smaller job," he said.

Luke sat at the auxiliary station with the salvage estimates open on his knees. He and Artoo had joined the Falcon twelve hours earlier at Ackbar's staging vessel. Luke had slept through most of the journey. Han had tried to, and found himself listening to the ship instead.

"Three possible custody beacons," Luke said. "Two are empty mounting assemblies. The third is in that cluster."

He pointed toward a group of returns beyond the main debris band.

Han enlarged it. A ragged frame turned slowly around an invisible center. Something once cylindrical lay caught inside it. Two smaller objects followed the frame through the same rotation, connected by lengths of cable too fine for the sensors to resolve.

"Of course it is."

Chewbacca made a low observation from the copilot's seat.

"No, I wasn't hoping it would be in the easy one. I was keeping an open mind."

The Wookiee gave him the look that usually accompanied that particular explanation.

Luke leaned forward between them. "Ackbar's team won't put a shuttle inside the cluster. The radiation goes up every time the broken reactor shield turns toward us."

Han watched the frame complete another fraction of its rotation. The notary pod was designed to leave the station in an emergency. Its little escape motor had done that much, then driven it into something larger moving almost the same way. Three months of patient orbital motion had twisted the collision into a knot.

"How long?"

Artoo projected a small red arc on the display.

"On this pass?" Luke said. "Nine minutes before we need the Falcon's shielding between us and the source."

Han checked the retrieval bay's external cameras. Chewbacca had fitted its opening with the extra shielding at the staging point. It looked like an ugly patch on an already ugly ship.

"Then let's make the first eight count."

***

They found the other salvagers when a cutting flare lit the frame from inside.

It was a precise green spark, followed by a fan of cooling metal. A small tug emerged behind it, edging backward on four widely separated thrusters. Its bow carried a cutting claw. Its transponder identified it as a sanitation tender licensed to clear military navigation hazards.

Han studied the tug's dorsal gun.

"Busy sanitation business."

He opened the channel Ackbar had assigned to the salvage operation.

"Tug inside the marked exclusion area, this is the Millennium Falcon. Shut down your cutters. You've got a radiation source turning toward you."

The tug continued backing away. Its claw was attached to one of the cables running through the frame.

Luke watched the growing angle between them.

"They're pulling the pod out."

"They're pulling the whole knot apart."

Han raised his voice.

"Listen to me. Let that cable go."

A man answered, breathing hard.

"Withdraw from the recovery boundary. This material is under executive custody."

"I'm sure it is. We're here to check the receipts."

The tug's gun moved toward them.

Han lowered the Falcon beneath the frame before it fired. Red bolts passed overhead. One struck an old docking collar, throwing off a glittering sheet of fragments. Chewbacca brought their shields up, and the fragments swept across them with a patter Han could feel through his boots.

"All right," he said. "We can do it the other way."

He brought the bow around far enough to put the tug on the forward targeting display. Its engines were exposed beneath the aft hull. One burst would stop it pulling.

Luke caught his wrist.

"There's someone outside."

Han looked again.

A pale shape moved along the tensioned cable between the tug and the wreckage. A worker in a pressure suit, no bigger than Han's thumb on the screen. The worker was trying to reach a mounting point on the frame. The cable shivered with every engine correction.

The tug fired again.

Han took his hand off the trigger.

"I see him."

He pushed the Falcon farther down and around, keeping the great curve of a shattered coolant pipe between them and the gun. Chewbacca brought up the retrieval field. Its indicator flashed amber.

The Wookiee growled.

"What do you mean the clamp won't unfold?"

A sharp reply.

Han could hear the strain already, somewhere aft. Something the extra shielding had pressed out of alignment.

Luke was unfastening his harness.

"I'll get the worker."

Han looked at the pale shape on the cable. The person had stopped moving forward.

"Take the second tether."

"I will."

"And take Artoo. We don't have time for two trips."

***

The stars turned when Luke left the airlock.

For a moment he felt the movement in his stomach rather than his eyes: the Falcon matching the wreckage's rotation, the frame moving across the moon, the suit's small maneuvering jets working against his shoulder blades. He fixed his attention on the worker. Beyond that pressure helmet was a frightened living presence, close enough for him to feel its struggle without knowing the thoughts behind it.

Artoo came out after him in a simple thruster cradle. He protested at the tether attached to his dome.

"You can complain when we're inside," Luke said.

The droid answered with a descending whistle.

"Inside the Falcon."

Luke caught a handrail on the frame. It moved beneath his glove, transmitting a deep vibration. He clipped the second tether, checked Artoo's connection, and pushed toward the worker.

The cable snapped before he reached him.

There was no sound. A section of silver line whipped past Luke's faceplate, struck the handrail, and disappeared into space. The worker spun away with both arms extended. A torn hose trailed from the suit's side.

Luke reached into the Force.

The man's motion slowed. He was still rotating, but the widening distance between them stopped increasing. Luke drew him back carefully, using his jets to meet him. The worker struck his shoulder hard enough to push the air from his lungs.

They turned together. Luke found the man's harness and locked a tether onto it.

"I have you," he said. "Stop firing your pack."

The worker's boots kicked against his. Through the helmet Luke saw a face with its eyes squeezed shut.

"Look at me."

The eyes opened.

Luke put a gloved hand over the torn hose and squeezed the suit's emergency clamp. A warning light changed from red to yellow.

"You have reserve air. Follow the line back to the ship."

"They're leaving," the man said.

Luke turned.

The tug had cut loose. It was backing out of the cluster, its gun turning after the Falcon. The freed frame had begun a new rotation. A great sheet of metal folded slowly across the space they had just occupied.

"Han."

"Working on it."

The Falcon surged across their view, putting its upper shields between the worker and the tug. Fire washed blue across the saucer. Luke heard Chewbacca roar over the open comm, followed by a crash from somewhere inside the ship.

Then the Falcon's retrieval arm opened.

"There it is," Han said. "Chewie, you're beautiful."

The tug turned away. Its pilot had evidently decided that leaving quickly mattered more than whatever the worker might say.

Luke brought the rescued man to the frame's lee side and clipped him to the line leading toward the Falcon. Artoo waited beside a cylindrical object wedged through a broken truss. On its side, beneath a layer of pitting and scorched insulation, was the number Leia had sent.

Nineteen.

***

Han did not follow the tug.

He watched its engine trace retreat toward the debris band's edge and hated the calculation that let it go. The Falcon could have caught it in open space. Luke and Artoo were outside, the radiation arc was moving steadily across the display, and the frame was now turning on an axis none of their estimates had predicted.

Chewbacca hauled himself back into the cockpit, one forearm blackened with lubricant. He had freed the retrieval clamp by entering its service recess while the gunfire struck the shields on the other side.

"Nice work," Han said.

The Wookiee shoved him firmly back toward the controls.

"Right. Compliments later."

They followed Luke's tether toward the pod. Han could see where it was caught. Two folded truss members had pinched the pod's mounting collar, and a tangle of cable held the far end against the frame. Pulling would drag several hundred tons of wreckage after it.

"Luke, you've got to cut the collar."

"I see it. Artoo needs another minute."

"He can have half."

A protest came down the comm.

"You can have the whole minute if you come back and fly this."

Han reduced thrust. The Falcon slipped closer to the turning frame, close enough that a sensor mast passed above the cockpit windows. Chewbacca made a delicate adjustment to the retrieval field. The pod's cylindrical body shifted.

Artoo had opened a hatch near its end. His dome was almost hidden inside it. Luke braced one boot against a truss member, helping him hold the access panel clear.

The rescued worker reached the Falcon's airlock. The outer indicator changed as the ship took him in.

One fewer person outside.

The radiation warning sounded.

Han switched it off. He knew where the red arc was.

"Luke."

"Almost."

A new glow appeared in the pod, faint and blue. Artoo drew back, holding something in his gripper. Luke took it, placed it in a pouch, and reached into the hatch himself. A second object emerged, longer and carefully wrapped in an old protective sheath.

"That's enough," Han said.

Artoo extended his interface arm again.

Luke's voice came sharply over the comm.

"No, Artoo. We have to go."

The nearest truss member moved.

It came down with the terrible slowness of a large object already carrying more momentum than anything around it could stop. Han could see the narrowing space between it and the pod. Luke was in that space.

Chewbacca caught Han's shoulder and pointed.

The broken mounting collar.

Han understood. He rolled the Falcon, pushing one edge of its shields against the loose end of the truss. The shields flared. A grinding vibration ran through the ship. For a few seconds, the truss stopped closing.

Luke's green blade ignited. He cut once through the mounting collar, then caught Artoo's cradle and pushed off.

The pod sprang free.

Chewbacca pulled it toward the retrieval bay. The added weight dragged at the Falcon's turn. Han fought the controls, keeping their shielding across the radiation source while the frame came apart behind them.

"Luke, get clear!"

Two suit lights moved toward the airlock. One belonged to Luke. The other was Artoo's cradle beacon, spinning wildly until Luke caught it.

Han waited for the outer lock indicator. It stayed red.

The frame struck their lower shields.

The Falcon shuddered. A display went dark. Chewbacca held the retrieval field steady with both hands on its manual controller.

The lock indicator turned amber.

Han drove the engines forward.

They came out of the cluster trailing pieces of burned insulation and one very expensive damaged shield emitter. The moon filled the windows. Sunlight returned to the cockpit in a broad warm sheet.

Han let himself breathe.

"Everybody?"

Luke answered after a moment. "Everybody."

Artoo said something that did not sound grateful.

Chewbacca began to laugh.

***

The notary pod occupied most of the retrieval bay. Its outer shell had survived the explosion, the collision, and Han's extraction. The hatch Artoo had opened was now warped shut.

Luke laid the two recovered objects on the hold table. One was a rectangular memory core inside a shock cradle. The other, beneath its split sheath, was a small ceramic spindle with a cracked pale surface.

Han looked at it.

"We just risked my ship for pottery."

Luke ran a finger along the sheath's printed label without touching the spindle itself.

"And a droid's memory."

"A useful droid?"

Artoo made a pointed sound.

"I'm asking."

Luke smiled, but there was exhaustion in it. His shoulder was bruised where the worker had struck him, and the sleeve of his pressure suit had a long shallow score across it.

"The records inside the pod identify this as an original observation reference," he said. "It was being held for destruction. Artoo got the custody ledger and the paired seal, but the main archive's damaged. We couldn't bring the rest of it."

Han looked toward the retrieval bay. The whole notary pod had proved heavier than he had liked. Even if they cut it open, its remaining memory banks had been exposed when the collar tore free. The radiation readings made the decision simple.

"We'll let Ackbar's people take the shell. They can lose their hair working on it."

Chewbacca put a hand on his head.

"You know what I mean."

The rescued worker was sitting on a bunk with a thermal blanket around his shoulders. Han had left the door open. The man had already asked twice whether he was a prisoner.

Luke went to him now, while Han and Chewbacca examined the custody ledger Artoo projected above the table.

Two seals appeared side by side. The first recorded an executive wafer placed in a destruction packet. The second certified the arrival of that same packet at the notary. Their weights were different by a fraction too small for an ordinary cargo scale to notice.

Below them ran a brief transport exception.

Han read it twice.

The original wafer had left in a maintenance courier before the destruction packet arrived. Its movement was recorded as a recertification loan. The destruction packet carried the matching number and a returned blank.

"A receipt for the receipt," he said.

Chewbacca growled.

"Yeah. And somebody accepted it."

The maintenance courier's onward address was mostly damaged. Enough remained to identify a private medical logistics authority, the same trust Venn had been tracing on Bespin. Artoo isolated a current licensed support reference beside it.

It did not tell them where the Meridian was. It told them who paid to keep her working.

From the bunk, the worker said, "They said the old seals still counted."

Han turned.

The man was looking at the projection. Luke stood beside him, giving him room.

"Who said that?" Han asked.

"The recovery broker. He gave us the pod's number. Said it had to be cleared before the Republic teams moved the field."

"Did he give you his name?"

The worker shook his head.

"He paid through a clinic account. I thought it was abandoned medical property. Then the pilot got orders to cut loose."

His fingers tightened on the blanket.

Han glanced at the retreating tug's trace. They had sent it to the salvage patrol as soon as they were clear. Perhaps the patrol would catch it; perhaps the pilot knew a route through the outer debris that Han did not. The worker was here because Luke had spent their safe firing opportunity saving him.

"We'll keep you away from the broker," Han said.

The man looked toward the cockpit, where an encrypted link to Ackbar's staging vessel was waiting.

"I don't want my family contacted through that account."

Luke sat on the other end of the bunk.

"Then we'll find another way."

Han turned off the ledger projection. A line of afternoon light from Endor lay across the little spindle and the old droid's silent core. Somebody had brought them here to be destroyed under the Emperor's own seal. Instead, they were leaving in a smuggler's hold.

For once, the Emperor's plans had been poorly served by Han Solo getting in the way.

# Chapter Nineteen: The Men Behind the Throne

Imperial Palace, Coruscant. Shortly before the Emperor's departure for Endor.

Sim Aloo disliked the way the transport case had been placed on his desk.

There was nothing improper about it. The black surface had been cleaned, the household seal faced outward, and the courier had withdrawn without asking for a receipt. Yet the case occupied the space where Sim normally rested his hands. He had to stand in his own office while he decided whether to open it.

Janus Greejatus watched from beside the window.

"You could send it back."

"Would you?"

Greejatus adjusted a fold of his robe. He had dressed for the Emperor's forthcoming journey, though the departure was still hours away. Elaborate colors were useful armor in the household. Men could be made to look indispensable long after they had become unnecessary.

"I would find out who sent it."

"We know who sent it."

The case's sender field identified the Custodian's office.

Sim broke the outer seal. Inside lay an executive authorization wafer in a padded recess. Beside it was a thinner object with the same serial printed across its edge. The accompanying documents described a recertification exchange.

Greejatus came away from the window.

"That's a live device."

"For maintenance sessions. Limited scope."

"You sound like him."

Sim held the wafer between his thumb and forefinger. It was warm from the case, scarcely heavier than the household seals he used every day. Long ago, an adviser had needed the Emperor's ear. Now people could put part of that access into a piece of metal and send it through a service lift.

The room's private annunciator sounded. Sim closed his hand.

"Admit him."

Veyr entered without attendants. He wore plain dark robes, and carried a clerk's narrow document wallet. Greejatus did not offer him a chair.

"You sent an original to my office," Sim said.

"An issued original. It is already on your custody schedule."

"It is also due to accompany the Emperor."

"Its destruction receipt is due to accompany him."

Greejatus looked toward the open case.

"You had better explain the difference before I call the guard."

Veyr laid his wallet on the desk. He opened it to a routing document that bore Sim's own authorization.

"Your office approved the clinical maintenance exception last month. It permits recertification outside the notary's immediate custody. A continuity instrument remains valid until the returned object is reconciled."

"Of course, Majesty."

All of that was true.

He surprised himself by needing the assurance. Perhaps it would be useful later, if anyone asked which part of the arrangement he had intended.

Above it, the Emperor's shuttle received a private corridor through the traffic.

"When we return," Greejatus said, "we should reconsider our arrangement with that man."

Sim touched the place inside his robe where the wafer had rested. Without it, the fold seemed strangely empty.

"When we return," he agreed.

# Chapter Twenty-Two: The Emperor's Final Night

The second Death Star. Hours before the Battle of Endor.

The last courier shuttle was already gone when the Emperor ordered the hangars sealed.

Sim Aloo watched its departure code vanish from the traffic display. For an instant he could still see the little vessel through the observation window, turning across the immense unfinished curve of the station. Then it passed behind a construction spar. The green point beside its name became a grey one.

He moved his hand away from the display before the Emperor noticed where he had been looking. The shuttle carried a few personal reserves and the sealed accounts that might one day buy their owner a passage. The authorization wafer had taken a different route from Coruscant before the Emperor's departure. Sim had been careful never to put every means of escape aboard the same ship.

"A busy morning, Sim?"

Palpatine had not turned from the window. The forest moon hung below them, bright enough to cast a pale reflection on the polished floor. In the adjoining room, workers were removing the last of the instruments used to prepare his private audience chamber. They carried each piece with the exaggerated care of men who wished to become invisible.

"Routine supplies, Majesty. The construction office has requested authority for three further departures."

"Denied."

Sim lowered his eyes to the list. One of those ships was his. Its destination was a storage vessel beyond the edge of the fleet, where he had placed spare clothing, credit reserves and a small collection of objects that belonged, incontrovertibly, to him.

"The departures were approved yesterday."

"Yesterday I had not refused them."

"Of course."

"Clear the service bays. Military traffic only. No private vessels, no advisers' errands, no urgent discoveries that somebody has forgotten an indispensable possession on Coruscant."

Sim entered the instruction. A red border appeared around the departure schedule. The ships remaining in the bays were now little more than numbered rooms.

Palpatine turned. His gaze rested on the traffic display, and Sim felt the unpleasant certainty that the courier's grey point was still visible beneath the new instructions.

"You have already sent your personal effects ahead, I trust."

"Nothing of consequence, Majesty."

The Emperor studied him for a moment. Then he held out his hand.

Sim gave him the authorization slate.

They crossed into the unfinished operations room behind the audience chamber. Here the station still showed its bones. Bundles of temporary cable ran along the floor inside yellow guards. A technician knelt beside an open panel, testing the circuit that would connect the Emperor's chair to his private communications network. When Palpatine entered, the man tried to stand too quickly and struck his shoulder against the panel's edge.

"Continue," Palpatine said.

The technician returned to his work with both hands trembling.

A holographic image of Custodian Veyr waited above a small console. Signal interference made the edges of his sleeves shiver, although the man himself stood very still. On Coruscant it would be night. He had apparently dressed for the summons before it arrived.

"We will conclude this matter now," Palpatine told him.

Veyr inclined his head. "The cessation instructions are prepared."

"Prepared is a word you have employed for several days. I would prefer a different one."

Sim laid the slate on the console. Five administrative branches unfolded across its surface. Beneath each were acknowledgments from research officers, transport supervisors and medical custodians. Most carried the same small green mark.

Palpatine touched one.

"What does this signify?"

"Enrollment suspended," Veyr said. "No new subject may be entered. No new authority binding may be established."

"And this?"

"Active trials concluded."

The Emperor selected a third mark. A narrow line opened beneath it: CONTINUITY EXEMPTION.

Veyr answered before he was asked. "Patients requiring medical observation remain in care. Sudden withdrawal of their support systems would invalidate the cessation procedure."

Palpatine looked up. "Would it?"

There was no interest in the patients in that question. Sim could hear it clearly. The Emperor wanted to know which part of his instruction had acquired a meaning he had not given it.

"Several facilities share their environmental controllers with the reference apparatus," Veyr said. "We have separated the experimental permissions from the clinical services. The remaining exceptions preserve the latter."

That was almost true. Sim had inspected the arrangement himself. The permissions had different names, different administrative owners and different headings on the slate. In several crucial places they still depended upon the same maintenance sessions. Veyr had made an exemption large enough to shelter the work, then filled its entrance with patients whom no diligent clerk would dare to disconnect without further orders.

The Emperor returned the slate to the console.

"How many living witnesses?"

"The complete accounting is attached."

"I did not ask where it was attached."

Veyr gave the number. Sim watched the technician by the wall press a connector into place and withdraw his hand as though it might be burned. The man was trying not to listen. It made every word in the room seem louder.

Palpatine read the accounting without comment. Then he opened the final page.

It contained a command none of the green acknowledgments could execute. A physical notary unit aboard the station held the means to authenticate it, but the final confirmation belonged to the Emperor alone. Once issued and received by each local controller, it would end the active bindings, erase the writable authority index and make the recovered reference apparatus incapable of renewing a command session. The old observations could be retained under seal. Palpatine did not care who read a medical interview fifty years after everyone in it had ceased to matter.

He cared very much who could order a living person to move.

"This confirmation should be placed in a timed queue," Veyr said. "In the event that the battle interrupts communications, the work could still be completed."

Sim wished the scientist had kept silent.

Palpatine lifted his head slowly.

"In the event that I am unable to give an order, you would like my machinery to decide that I have given it."

"A restricted contingency, Majesty. It would do only what you have already instructed."

"Then it would do nothing. I have instructed you to await my confirmation."

The image of Veyr flickered. Sim could not tell whether the pause belonged to the signal or the man.

"Understood."

He rested one finger on the sealed page.

"No substitute. No presumption. No interpretation."

Veyr bowed. The movement carried his face through a broken line of blue light.

"You have my acknowledgment."

"I have several of your acknowledgments. Sim will verify what they mean."

The Emperor ended the connection himself.

For a few moments the room contained only the small noises of the technician finishing his work. A tool clicked against the floor. The man bent to retrieve it and remained crouched.

"The notary packet," Palpatine said.

Sim opened a narrow cabinet. Inside, under a transparent cover, lay the small dark memory core that had once belonged to Damask's household droid. Beside it, the ceramic spindle rested in its fitted reference cartridge, a protective sheath lined with the original calibration markings. Both objects had travelled far to become things in a cabinet on a battle station.

The droid had been dismantled years earlier. Its last intact memories had been kept because the machine's chain of custody was inconveniently thorough. Transfers, corrections, cancelled instructions and preserved observations all remained, each with the date at which a living official had attempted to change its meaning. "Keep it so."

Palpatine closed the cover. He did not issue the instruction.

A chime sounded at the communications desk. The technician rose this time without striking anything.

"My lord. The private circuit is ready. May I test the authority channel?"

Palpatine gave a slight movement of assent.

The man touched the panel. A familiar voice emerged from the room's speakers, calm and perfectly audible.

"You may proceed."

Sim stopped breathing.

It was the Emperor's voice. An old instruction, taken from the work authorization, reproduced exactly as it had been spoken earlier that morning. The technician's expression changed before the sound had finished.

Palpatine crossed the room. He did not hurry. The man moved backward until his shoulders touched the open panel.

"Why," the Emperor asked, "am I giving that instruction?"

"It is the circuit test, my lord. The stored approval confirms—"

The final word failed. The technician's lips remained parted. One hand rose to his throat.

Palpatine watched him struggle for enough breath to understand the question.

The man's knees bent. He shook his head.

"Did I give permission for anyone who presses that panel to proceed with whatever he chooses?"

Another desperate movement of the head.

Palpatine released him. The technician fell against the wall and slid to the floor, coughing. Sim kept his eyes on the cable guards. There were no qualifications on them, no exemptions, nothing that required him to decide whether he had seen too much.

The technician crawled toward the panel.

Palpatine turned to Sim. "This is how it begins. An obedient man keeps a useful scrap of authority. Another obedient man finds a different use for it. Eventually I discover I have been issuing orders in rooms I have never entered."

Sim could feel the empty place beneath his sleeve where the diverted wafer should have been.

He left the operations room. At the doorway he paused.

"Gone before the restriction."

Sim looked at him.

For years the two of them had preserved their lives by giving each other incomplete accounts of ordinary matters. Now Greejatus had suggested surrendering their one surviving advantage in order to obtain a ship they would almost certainly not be allowed to board.

Sim did not answer. Behind Greejatus, two naval officers passed carrying a case of communication components. They were talking about the coming fleet engagement. One of them sounded pleased.

Greejatus waited until they had gone.

Greejatus looked toward the lift again. Sim opened his door and allowed him inside.

"All right," she said. "Let's go somewhere."

"You mean that?"

"Yes."

He put his arm around her. She leaned against him and watched the terrace while the first evening lights appeared between the towers.

It would take time. There were names to restore, people to help, habits of fear that would outlast the machines that had cultivated them. Tomorrow she would return to the work. Tonight, a conversation was beginning at the nearest table, and somebody had left room for them.

Han took her empty cup.

Together they went to sit down.
